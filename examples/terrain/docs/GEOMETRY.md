# Geometry and materials

CYBR GEO assembles the surface, exposed layer walls, water, and recorded display detail into a metric scene. The surface comes from the saved column height and material stack. Fine render detail adds sub-grid relief and fragments without changing the simulation file or silently changing its ledger.

The surface detail now has three scales: centimetric weathering on the actual surface mesh, angular fracture fragments with planar normals, and independent soil aggregates with millimetric to centimetric silhouettes. Fragment placement depends on substrate, slope, and water; extra close-view detail is explicitly recorded in scene metadata. These particles are render detail, not newly simulated sediment.

Seeded periodic 512-pixel atlases describe aggregate clods, mineral grains, pores, mineral flecks, and shale bedding over a 1.25-metre material period. Linear albedo, tangent-space normal, and absolute roughness use the same underlying material field. Wetness darkens albedo, reduces roughness, and fills the smallest pore relief while keeping the dry/wet aggregate pattern consistent. World-space vertex pigment reduces repeated tiles. Triangles crossing a material or moisture contact and a two-display-cell halo share one clamped world-UV PBR material. A chunked 2048-pixel bake blends the periodic albedo in linear space, normal vectors and absolute roughness with bounded exposure weights. Actual deposition coverage/composition supplies continuous pigment; partial coatings retain the substrate microtexture, and fully covered loose beds use their own grain material. Matching the interior pigment formula prevents another seam at the outer halo. Its normals preserve physical slope rather than changing strength with atlas scale. Interiors retain their 512-pixel periodic materials, and the original surface triangles, height, normals and simulated inventories remain unchanged.

Native color, normal and roughness samplers clamp this world atlas; the GLB assigns matching clamp samplers. Surface parts share one object ID for display-guide filtering, so material partitions do not introduce artificial denoising borders. Stones, cut faces and the closed dielectric water region keep their own identities. The baked atlas trades some fine texture resolution for continuous geological contacts, especially on large sites.

Thin deposited films keep the underlying bed texture and apply a pigment coverage proportional to their actual bulk depth and transported gravel/sand/fines mixture. Full loose-bed material coverage starts at `max(0.01 m, 0.5 × substrate grain scale)`. Those optical thresholds and coverage fractions are recorded; they do not change height or solid inventories.

The exported GLB includes UVs and embedded PBR textures. CYBR LIGHT uses the same geometry and linear material textures with its native spectral plastic and dielectric BSDFs. CYBR LIGHT samples the roughness texture per hit; material texture/tint and normal-map shading are also evaluated in first-hit display guides. Attribute-preserving meshlets carry positions, normals, UVs, vertex color, material IDs, and object IDs. The current CPU path builds a BVH over meshlet-referenced triangles; meshlets are an interchange and locality structure, not a claim of GPU mesh-shader execution.

The native roughness texture's red channel is absolute perceptual roughness in `[0,1]`, used consistently by GGX sampling and evaluation. Explicit anisotropic alpha values take precedence. First-hit albedo guides integrate the actual spectral reflectance, including UV texture and vertex tint; mapped-normal guides recurse through normal/bump wrappers. The display filter uses albedo, normal, depth, object, and variance edges. Native hemisphere checks prevent a mapped normal from admitting reflective illumination through the back of the actual terrain surface.

Saved native output includes the scene recipe, meshlets, linear film, and diagnostic guides. Display filtering does not rewrite the linear film. A close view is useful for judging grain-scale material structure; the profile view exposes layer thicknesses intentionally.

Assets are stored under `assets/<recipe hash>/`, keyed by canonical state contents, geometry/material/lighting source, subdivision, and scatter budget. A completed asset directory is checked against a private regeneration and is never overwritten. Scene dependencies point into that recipe. Each view receipt hashes only its native files, its immutable assets, and a matching simulation state; it excludes other views and mutable latest-model aliases. The receipt names the authoritative GLB and saved state.
